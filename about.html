Attuning, without forcing or controlling.

I’m a scholar and a practitioner of embodied and relational well-being. Beneath everything I do is a rather Taoist approach: deep trust in, and support of, the inherent self-organizing principle of life, oriented toward health, development, integration and vitality.

  • 15years of bodywork practice
  • 18years of contact improvisation, including teaching at Texas A&M University
  • 10years of relational and intersubjective facilitation
  • McGillMaster’s thesis on embodiment, relationship and whole-systems well-being

My training runs back to Ida Rolf and Milton Trager.

I attended IPSB, the International Professional School of Bodywork, where at the center of my training in a variety of modalities I completed the Structural Integration program in 2011. The school emphasizes awareness, movement, and an orientation toward pleasure.

I received both teaching and the ten sessions from Ed Maupin, and I studied under James Stewart. Through years of practice, the two lineages have woven together in my work.

Rolf named the objective of the ten-session series “Expansional Balance”: the unfolding of the spring-loaded response to gravity that our bodies evolved for.

I’ve practiced relational facilitation for ten years.

Alongside bodywork, I’m trained in a number of interpersonal growth and healing practices, and I’ve led and assisted groups in them.

  • I’ve practiced contact improvisation for eighteen years, including teaching at Texas A&M University.
  • I’m trained in Circling, a relational meditation and communication practice. I co-led the world’s first Circling and contact improvisation weekend workshop, at Earthdance Creative Living in Western Massachusetts.
  • In 2022, The Connection Institute tapped me to help lead a five-month group coaching training in Austin, Texas.
  • I assisted in UnTriggered, a workshop on nervous system co-regulation, in Asheville, North Carolina.
  • I’m also trained in Internal Family Systems therapy, and in a variety of breathwork called Integral Breath Therapy.

My research looks at how relationship shapes health.

At McGill University I completed a thesis demonstrating how the quality of our relationships to our own embodiment, to other humans, and to the more-than-human world, is a core determinant of human health and function.

It integrates findings from many disciplines: interoception, embodiment, mindfulness, the neuroscience of perception, psychophysiology, trauma, interpersonal neurobiology, self- and co-regulation, neuroplasticity, developmental psychology, attachment theory, ecological economics, and complex systems science; toward an understanding of whole-systems well-being, from person to planet.

I also have a Bachelor of Science in Ecological Restoration from Texas A&M.

Habits of Being brings both halves of my work together.

  • In the room: your ten Wednesday evenings with the group.
